# World Cantonese Dialects & Diachronic Evolution (Variation Layer)

## 1. The Yue Linguistic Sphere (大灣區與海外粵語)
Cantonese is the prestige variety of the Yue Chinese branch, spoken natively by over 85 million people across Hong Kong, Macau, Guangdong, eastern Guangxi, and historic global Chinatowns:

1. **Hong Kong Cantonese (香港粵語)**:
   - Modern cultural and media powerhouse standard.
   - Characterized by rapid lexical innovation, extensive English code-switching and loanwords (*波* ball, *士多* store, *拜拜* bye-bye, *卡* card, *拍拖* pak-tor), and the prevalence of Lazy Pronunciation (*平話 / 懶音*):
     - Initial merger: /n-/ $\to$ [l-] (*你* *nei5* pronounced *lei5*; *男* *naam4* pronounced *laam4*).
     - Velar nasal drop: /ng-/ $\to$ [$\varnothing$-] (*我* *ngo5* pronounced *o5*).
     - Velar coda glide: /-ng/ and /-k/ merging into dental /-n/ and /-t/ after certain vowels.
2. **Guangzhou Standard (廣州話)**:
   - Historical phonetic standard with strict distinction of /n-/ vs /l-/ and /ng-/ vs null onset.
   - Preserves traditional idiom structures with less Western loanword influence.
3. **Siyi / Taishanese (四邑話 / 台山話)**:
   - Archaic Yue varieties spoken in Jiangmen/Taishan; 5 tones, distinctive /ɬ/ lateral fricative, phonologically distinct from Guangzhou/Hong Kong.
4. **Malaysian / Singaporean Cantonese (星馬粵語)**:
   - Infused with Malay borrowings (*balik* $\to$ *巴力* return, *saman* fine) and English corporate terms.

## 2. Diachronic Evolution from Middle Chinese
Cantonese is celebrated as one of the most phonologically conservative Sinitic varieties:
- **Checked Codas (入聲)**: Preserves the tripartite Middle Chinese unreleased stop codas **-p, -t, -k** (lost entirely in Standard Mandarin).
- **Bilabial Nasal Coda (-m)**: Preserves Middle Chinese final *-m* (e.g. *三* *saam1*, *心* *sam1*; merged into *-n* in Mandarin).
- **Vowel Distinctions**: Retains contrast between long /aː/ and short /ɐ/ (*三* *saam1* vs *心* *sam1*).
