# Arabic Engine — Layer 2: Morphological Analysis (Word Layer)

## 1. Non-Concatenative Root-and-Pattern Architecture

Semitic morphology departs from linear concatenation. Words are generated by interweaving a lexical **Root** (consonantal skeleton carrying semantic family) into a morphological **Pattern / Template** (vocalic and affixal frame carrying grammatical meaning).

$$\text{Word} = \text{Root} \otimes \text{Pattern}$$

Example: Root $\sqrt{k-t-b}$ (ك-ت-ب: writing):
| Pattern | Vocalization | Resulting Word | Semantic Function |
|---------|--------------|----------------|-------------------|
| $Fa'ala$ | $a-a-a$ | *kataba* (كَتَبَ) | Form I Verb: "He wrote" |
| $Kātib$ | $\bar{a}-i$ | *kātib* (كَاتِب) | Active Participle: "Writer / scribe" |
| $Kitāb$ | $i-\bar{a}$ | *kitāb* (كِتَاب) | Noun: "Book" |
| $Maktab$ | $ma--a$ | *maktab* (مَكْتَب) | Noun of Place: "Office / desk" |
| $Maktaba$ | $ma--a-a$ | *maktaba* (مَكْتَبَة) | Noun of Place (Collective): "Library / bookstore" |
| $Maktūb$ | $ma--\bar{u}$ | *maktūb* (مَكْتُوب) | Passive Participle: "Written / letter" |

## 2. Derived Verb Forms I–X (الأَوْزَانُ العَشَرَةُ)

Arabic standardizes 10 major derived verbal templates operating on triconsonantal roots:

| Form | Past (Māḍī) | Present (Muḍāri') | Verbal Noun (Maṣdar) | Core Semantic Nuance | Example Root $\sqrt{k-t-b}$ / $\sqrt{'-l-m}$ |
|------|-------------|-------------------|----------------------|----------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| **I** | فَعَلَ (*fa'ala*) | يَفْعُلُ (*yaf'ulu*) | فَعْل / فِعَالَة | Ground stem | *kataba* (كَتَبَ - wrote) |
| **II** | فَعَّلَ (*fa''ala*) | يُفَعِّلُ (*yufa''ilu*) | تَفْعِيل (*taf'īl*) | Intensive / Causative | *'allama* (عَلَّمَ - taught) |
| **III** | فَاعَلَ (*fā'ala*) | يُفَاعِلُ (*yufā'ilu*) | مُفَاعَلَة (*mufā'ala*) | Associative / Reciprocal | *kātaba* (كَاتَبَ - corresponded) |
| **IV** | أَفْعَلَ (*'af'ala*) | يُفْعِلُ (*yuf'ilu*) | إِفْعَال (*'if'āl*) | Transitive / Causative | *'arsala* (أَرْسَلَ - sent) |
| **V** | تَفَعَّلَ (*tafa''ala*) | يَتَفَعَّلُ (*yatafa''alu*) | تَفَعُّل (*tafa''ul*) | Reflexive of Form II | *ta'allama* (تَعَلَّمَ - learned) |
| **VI** | تَفَاعَلَ (*tafā'ala*) | يَتَفَاعَلُ (*yatafā'alu*) | تَفَاعُل (*tafā'ul*) | Mutual reciprocity | *takātaba* (تَكَاتَبَ - exchanged letters) |
| **VII** | اِنْفَعَلَ (*infa'ala*) | يَنْفَعِلُ (*yanfa'ilu*) | اِنْفِعَال (*infi'āl*) | Medio-passive | *inkasara* (اِنْكَسَرَ - broke) |
| **VIII** | اِفْتَعَلَ (*ifta'ala*) | يَفْتَعِلُ (*yafta'ilu*) | اِفْتِعَال (*ifti'āl*) | Reflexive / Participatory | *ijtama'a* (اِجْتَمَعَ - gathered) |
| **IX** | اِفْعَلَّ (*if'alla*) | يَفْعَلُّ (*yaf'allu*) | اِفْعِلَال (*if'ilāl*) | Colors & defects | *ihmarra* (اِحْمَرَّ - turned red) |
| **X** | اِسْتَفْعَلَ (*istaf'ala*) | يَسْتَفْعِلُ (*yastaf'ilu*) | اِسْتِفْعَال (*istif'āl*) | Seeking / Requesting | *istakhraja* (اِسْتَخْرَجَ - extracted) |

## 3. Nominal Pluralization Dynamics

Arabic utilizes two contrasting plural architectures:

### 3.1 Sound Plurals (الجَمْعُ السَّالِمُ - al-Jam' as-Sālim)
Suffixation that preserves the internal singular stem intact:
- **Sound Masculine Plural**: *-ūna* (Nominative) / *-īna* (Accusative/Genitive) (e.g., *mu'allim* $\to$ *mu'allimūna / mu'allimīna*).
- **Sound Feminine Plural**: *-āt* (e.g., *mu'allima* $\to$ *mu'allimāt*).

### 3.2 Broken Plurals (جَمْعُ التَّكْسِيرِ - Jam' at-Taksīr)
Templatic internal vowel apophony that re-vocalizes the consonantal root:
- $Fu'ūl$ (فُعُول): *qalb* $\to$ *qulūb*, *dars* $\to$ *durūs*
- $Fu'ul$ (فُعُل): *kitāb* $\to$ *kutub*, *safīna* $\to$ *sufun*
- $'Af'āl$ (أَفْعَال): *walad* $\to$ *'awlād*, *qalam* $\to$ *'aqlām*
- $Mafā'il$ (مَفَاعِل): *maktab* $\to$ *makātib*, *masjid* $\to$ *masājid*
- $Mafā'īl$ (مَفَاعِيل): *miftāh* $\to$ *mafātīh*.

## 4. Case Voweling (الإِعْرَابُ - al-I'rāb)

Standard Arabic enforces a 3-case system:
1. **Nominative (مَرْفُوعٌ - Marfū')**: Marked by *ḍamma* (ـُ) or *tanwīn ḍamm* (ـٌ). Governs Subject, Predicate, and Agent.
2. **Accusative (مَنْصُوبٌ - Manṣūb)**: Marked by *fatḥa* (ـَ) or *tanwīn fatḥ* (ـً). Governs Direct Object, Adverbials, and Predicate of *kāna*.
3. **Genitive (مَجْرُورٌ - Majrūr)**: Marked by *kasra* (ـِ) or *tanwīn kasr* (ـٍ). Governs Objects of Prepositions and Mudāf Ilayh.
4. **Diptotes (المَمْنُوعُ مِنَ الصَّرْفِ - al-Mamnū' min aṣ-Ṣarf)**: Defective declension taking *ḍamma* in Nominative, *fatḥa* in both Accusative and Genitive, and never accepting *tanwīn*.
