# Arabic Engine — Layer 5: Pragmatics & Register (Use Layer)

## 1. Greetings & Asymmetric Politeness Protocol

Arabic interpersonal interaction is strictly mediated through codified ceremonial adjacency pairs:

### 1.1 The Universal Islamic Salutation
- **Call**: *As-Salāmu 'Alaykum wa-Raḥmatullāhi wa-Barakātuh* (السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَةُ اللهِ وَبَرَكَاتُهُ)
  "Peace be upon you and the mercy of God and His blessings."
- **Obligatory Canonical Response**: *Wa-'Alaykum As-Salāmu wa-Raḥmatullāhi wa-Barakātuh* (وَعَلَيْكُمُ السَّلَامُ وَرَحْمَةُ اللهِ وَبَرَكَاتُهُ)
  "And upon you be peace and the mercy of God and His blessings."

### 1.2 Diurnal Greetings & Light Response Suffixation
- Morning: *Ṣabāḥ al-Khayr* (صَبَاحُ الخَيْرِ - Morning of goodness) $\longrightarrow$ **Ṣabāḥ an-Nūr** (صَبَاحُ النُّورِ - Morning of light).
- Evening: *Masā' al-Khayr* (مَسَاءُ الخَيْرِ - Evening of goodness) $\longrightarrow$ **Masā' an-Nūr** (مَسَاءُ النُّورِ - Evening of light).
- Welcome: *Ahlan wa-Sahlan* (أَهْلاً وَسَهْلاً) $\longrightarrow$ **Ahlan bika** (masc) / **Ahlan biki** (fem).

## 2. Institutional Honorific Titles (أَلْقَابُ التَّشْرِيفِ)

Administrative, legal, and academic correspondence mandates formal honorific prefixes:

| Title | Context / Social Rank | Interlinear Meaning |
|-------|----------------------|---------------------|
| **فَخَامَة (*Fakhāmat*)** | Heads of State / Presidents | "Excellency / Splendor" |
| **مَعَالِي (*Ma'ālī*)** | Cabinet Ministers / Ambassadors | "Highness / Eminence" |
| **سَعَادَة (*Sa'ādat*)** | Directors / Undersecretaries | "Happiness / Honor" |
| **فَضِيلَة (*Faḍīlat*)** | Grand Muftis / Religious Scholars | "Virtue" |
| **سَمَاحَة (*Samāḥat*)** | High Jurisprudents / Dignitaries | "Magnanimity" |
| **الأُسْتَاذُ الدُّكْتُور (*al-'Ustādh ad-Duktūr*)**| University Professors | "Professor Doctor" |
| **السَّيِّد / السَّيِّدَة (*as-Sayyid / as-Sayyida*)**| Respectful General Address | "Mr. / Mrs." |

## 4. Diglossia Management: Fus'ha vs. 'Āmmiyya

Arabic speech communities exist in functional diglossia:
- **Modern Standard Arabic (الفُصْحَى - Fus'ha)**: Used exclusively for written literature, legal administration, formal academia, news broadcasts, and international contracts.
- **Colloquial Dialects (العَامِّيَّةُ - 'Āmmiyya)**: Spoken domestic vernaculars (Egyptian, Levantine, Gulf, Maghrebi) lacking nominal case endings (*i'rāb*) and employing non-standard verbal pre-fixes (e.g. Egyptian *bi-*, Levantine *'am*).
- The Arabic Engine enforces pure MSA standards, auditing text for inappropriate colloquial incursions in formal registers.
