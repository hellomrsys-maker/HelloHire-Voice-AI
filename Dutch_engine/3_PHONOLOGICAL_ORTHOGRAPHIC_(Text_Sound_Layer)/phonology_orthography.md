# Dutch Phonological & Orthographic Specification (Text & Sound Layer)

## 1. Phonological Inventory

### 1.1 Consonants
The Dutch consonant inventory consists of:
- **Plosives**: /p, b, t, d, k, (ɡ)/ (Note: /ɡ/ occurs primarily in loanwords or via regressive voice assimilation before voiced stops, e.g. *zakdoek* [ˈzɑɡduk]).
- **Fricatives**: /f, v, s, z, x, ɣ, χ, ɦ/ (Glottal /ɦ/ replaces /h/; velar/uvular fricatives vary regionally).
- **Nasals**: /m, n, ŋ/
- **Liquids & Glides**: /l, r, ʋ, j/

### 1.2 Vowels & Diphthongs
Dutch features a rich vowel system with an essential distinction between **tense (long)** and **lax (short)** vowels:
- **Short (Lax) Vowels**: /ɪ/ (*wit*), /ɛ/ (*bed*), /ɔ/ (*kat* - back open /ɑ/), /ɔ/ (*bos*), /ʏ/ (*bus*).
- **Long (Tense) Vowels**: /i/ (*dier*), /y/ (*duur*), /u/ (*boek*), /eː/ (*been*), /øː/ (*deur*), /oː/ (*boom*), /aː/ (*maan*).
- **Diphthongs**:
  - Genuine Diphthongs: /ɛi/ (*ei / ij*), /œy/ (*ui*), /ɔu/ (*ou / au*).
  - Marginal/Loan Diphthongs: /ɑi/ (*haai*), /oːi/ (*mooi*), /iu/ (*nieuw*), /yu/ (*eeuw*).

## 2. Orthographic Principles & Spelling Rules

### 2.1 Open vs. Closed Syllable Rule (Vowel Length & Consonant Doubling)
Dutch orthography strictly reflects phonological vowel length through syllable boundary mechanics:
1. **Closed Syllable with Short Vowel**: A single consonant follows a single vowel:
   - *bom* /bɔm/ (bomb) -> Plural: *bommen* /ˈbɔmən/ (double consonant keeps the first syllable closed and vowel short).
2. **Open Syllable with Long Vowel**: A single vowel letter at the end of a syllable is pronounced long:
   - *boom* /boːm/ (tree) -> Plural: *bomen* /ˈboːmən/ (single vowel letter *o* in open syllable *bo-men* represents long /oː/).
3. **Closed Syllable with Long Vowel**: Written with doubled vowel letters:
   - *maan* /maːn/, *been* /beːn/, *boot* /boːt/, *uur* /yːr/.

### 2.2 Final Devoicing (*Auslautverhärtung*)
All voiced obstruents (/b, d, v, z, ɣ/) are neutralized to their voiceless counterparts (/p, t, f, s, x/) in syllable codas:
- *hond* /ɦɔnd/ -> Phonetically [ɦɔnt] (Plural: *honden* [ˈɦɔndə(n)]).
- *ik heb* /ɦɛb/ -> Phonetically [ɪk ɦɛp] (Infinitive: *hebben* [ˈɦɛbə(n)]).
- *huis* /ɦœys/ (Plural: *huizen* [ˈɦœyzə(n)]).
- *graaf* /ɣraːf/ (Plural: *graven* [ˈɣraːvə(n)]).

### 2.3 The *IJ* Digraph Capitalization Invariant
The digraph **IJ** is treated as a single letter in Dutch orthography:
- When capitalized at the start of a sentence or proper noun, **both letters must be uppercase**:
  - Correct: *IJsselmeer*, *IJmuiden*, *IJsland*, *IJzeren gordijn*.
  - Incorrect: \*Ijsselmeer, \*Ijmuiden, \*Ijsland.

### 2.4 Guttural Fricatives: Hard Northern /χ/ vs. Soft Southern /ɣ/
- **Northern Dutch (Randstad / Northern provinces)**:
  - Strong uvular /χ/ for both *ch* and *g* (voiceless uvular fricative).
- **Southern Dutch & Flemish (Brabant, Limburg, Flanders)**:
  - Palato-velar "zachte g" (/ʝ/ or /ɣ/ voiced, distinct from voiceless /ç/ or /x/).

### 2.5 Word-Final *-n* Deletion (*Schwa Apocope*)
In standard spoken Northern Dutch, the word-final *-n* in unstressed inflectional endings (*-en*) is elided in standard colloquial speech:
- *lopen* [ˈloːpə]
- *boeken* [ˈbukə]
- Retained before vowels in liaison or formal declamatory registers.
