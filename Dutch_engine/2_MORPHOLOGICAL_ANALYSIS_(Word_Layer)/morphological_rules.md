# Morphological Analysis & Derivation for Dutch (Word Layer)
## Sovereign Engine: `Dutch_engine` | Diminutives & Gender Split

### 1. The Productive Diminutive System (*Verkleinwoorden*)
Diminutive derivation in Dutch is universally productive and encodes physical smallness, endearment, familiarity, or triviality.

#### A. Allomorphic Realization Rules

| Suffix | Phonological Trigger | Base Example | Diminutive Form | Gloss |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **-tje** | Stems ending in vowel, diphthong, or sonorants (*l, r, n*) preceded by long vowel | *stoel* (chair)<br>*vrouw* (woman)<br>*trein* (train) | *stoeltje*<br>*vrouwtje*<br>*treintje* | "little chair"<br>"little woman"<br>"little train" |
| **-je** | Stems ending in voiceless plosives/fricatives (*p, t, k, s, f*) | *boek* (book)<br>*kop* (cup)<br>*brief* (letter) | *boekje*<br>*kopje*<br>*briefje* | "booklet"<br>"little cup"<br>"note / ticket" |
| **-pje** | Stems ending in *m* preceded by long vowel or liquid (*l, r*) | *boom* (tree)<br>*bloem* (flower)<br>*film* (film) | *boompje*<br>*bloempje*<br>*filmpje* | "little tree"<br>"little flower"<br>"short video / clip" |
| **-etje** | Stems with short vowel + single sonorant (*m, n, l, r, ng*) | *bal* (ball)<br>*man* (man)<br>*ding* (thing) | *balletje*<br>*mannetje*<br>*dingetje* | "little ball"<br>"little man"<br>"little thing" |
| **-kje** | Stems ending in unstressed *-ing* | *woning* (residence)<br>*ketting* (chain) | *woninkje*<br>*kettinkje* | "little house"<br>"little necklace" |

#### B. The Absolute Neuter Gender Invariant
**Every diminutive noun in the Dutch language without exception is grammatically neuter and obligatorily takes the definite article *het*:**
- *de tafel* (common) $\to$ **het tafeltje** (neuter)
- *de man* (common) $\to$ **het mannetje** (neuter)
- *de stad* (common) $\to$ **het stadje** (neuter)

### 2. Adjective Declension & Inflectional Concord
Adjectives inflect with suffix *-e* or take zero ending based on gender, definiteness, and number:

| Syntactic Context | Example (Common: *man*) | Example (Neuter: *huis*) | Rule Applied |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definite Singular (*de / het*)** | *de oude man* | *het oude huis* | Add *-e* |
| **Demonstrative / Possessive** | *mijn oude man* | *dit oude huis* | Add *-e* |
| **Indefinite Singular (*een*)** | *een oude man* | **een oud huis** | **Neuter Indefinite: Zero ending!** |
| **Plural (Definite or Indefinite)** | *oude mannen* | *oude huizen* | Add *-e* |
| **Predicative Position** | *De man is oud.* | *Het huis is oud.* | Zero ending (bare root) |

*Error Invariant: Using inflected -e on an indefinite singular neuter noun (\*een mooie huis) is an ungrammatical error.*

### 3. Verb Conjugation: The *'t Kofschip* Rule for Weak Preterite
In regular weak verbs, past tense suffix (*-te* vs *-de*) and past participle ending (*-t* vs *-d*) is governed by the final consonant of the verbal stem:
- If the stem ends in a consonant of **'t kofschip** (*t, k, f, s, ch, p*): Suffix is **-te / -ten**; past participle ends in **-t**:
  - *werken* (stem *werk-*) $\to$ *werkte, gewerkt*.
  - *hopen* (stem *hoop-*) $\to$ *hoopte, gehoopt*.
- All other consonants take **-de / -den**; past participle ends in **-d**:
  - *leren* (stem *leer-*) $\to$ *leerde, geleerd*.
  - *leven* (stem *leef-* from *lev-*) $\to$ *leefde, geleefd* (voiced underlying root /v/).
