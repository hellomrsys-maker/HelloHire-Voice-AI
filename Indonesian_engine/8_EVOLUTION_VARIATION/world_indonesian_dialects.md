# Layer 8: Evolution & Variation (World Indonesian & Dialects) — Indonesian Language Engine

## 1. The Language Spectrum: Bahasa Baku vs Bahasa Gaul
Indonesian sociolinguistic reality is defined by a dynamic continuum between the high formal standard and the colloquial vernacular:
1. **Bahasa Baku (Standard Indonesian)**:
   - Prescribed by Badan Bahasa and used in official governance, legislation, education, formal journalism, and scientific literature.
   - Enforces full prefixation (*membeli, membaca, pekerjaan*), complete function words (*tidak, sudah, sangat*), and standard morphology.
2. **Bahasa Gaul / Bahasa Sehari-hari (Colloquial Urban Indonesian)**:
   - Originating as Jakarta youth slang (Prokem) heavily influenced by Betawi, Javanese, and Sundanese substrates, now the lingua franca of national media and informal conversation across the archipelago.
   - Key lexical transformations:
     - Negation: *tidak* $\to$ *nggak / gak / kagak*.
     - Degree: *sangat / amat* $\to$ *banget*.
     - Aspect: *sudah* $\to$ *udah*, *belum* $\to$ *belom*.
     - Pronouns: *saya / anda* $\to$ *gue / lu*.
     - Causative: *membuat / membikin* $\to$ *bikin*.
     - Verbal prefix reduction: *meN-* dropped or replaced with *N-* (*membeli* $\to$ *beli*, *melihat* $\to$ *ngeliat*, *mengobrol* $\to$ *ngobrol*).

## 2. Regional Substrates & Dialects across the Archipelago
- **Javanese Substrate (Central/East Java)**: Honorific loans (*Mas, Mbak*), passive marker *di-* used with bare root without prefix, discourse particles (*lho, kok, toh, ya*).
- **Sundanese Substrate (West Java)**: Phonetic shift /f/ $\to$ /p/, polite address *Kang, Teh*.
- **Sumatran / Classical Riau Malay**: Close proximity to standard literary Malay, retention of conservative phonology.
- **Eastern Indonesian (Melayu Ambon, Kupang, Manado, Papua)**: SVO with high analytic simplification, possessive construction *[Possessor] + punya + [Possessed]* (*saya punya rumah* instead of *rumah saya*).

## 3. Mutual Intelligibility: Bahasa Indonesia vs Bahasa Melayu (Malaysia/Brunei/Singapore)
- While sharing identical 1972 EYD spelling unification and high mutual intelligibility, lexical divergence reflects colonial substrates:
  - Indonesian loans from Dutch/Javanese vs Malaysian loans from English/Arabic:
    - Office: *kantor* (Dutch *kantoor*) vs *pejabat*.
    - Car: *mobil* (Dutch *automobiel*) vs *kereta*.
    - Free/Vacation: *libur* vs *cuti*.
    - Policy: *kebijakan* vs *dasar*.
