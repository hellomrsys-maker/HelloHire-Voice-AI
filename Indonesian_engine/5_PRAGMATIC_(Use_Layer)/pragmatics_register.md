# Layer 5: Pragmatic (Use Layer) — Indonesian Language Engine

## 1. Social Deixis & Honorific Address Hierarchy
Indonesian personal address is deeply conditioned by social hierarchy, age, institutional status, and modesty (*kesantunan*):

### 1.1 Respectful / Hierarchical Address
Direct 2nd-person pronouns (*kamu, kau*) are avoided when speaking to elders, superiors, or in formal settings; kin terms and honorifics are substituted:
- **Bapak (Pak)**: Respectful address to adult men, officials, teachers, supervisors.
- **Ibu (Bu)**: Respectful address to adult women, teachers, mothers, superiors.
- **Mas / Mbak**: Javanese polite peer honorifics widely adopted in modern urban Indonesian (*Mas* for young adult men, *Mbak* for young adult women).
- **Kakak (Kak)**: Respectful address to older siblings or older peers in modern professional/digital contexts.

### 1.2 Neutral & Peer Pronouns
- **Anda**: Polite, neutral, egalitarian pronoun created post-independence for corporate, media, and official announcements.
- **Kamu**: Informal 2nd person singular for peers, close friends, or children.
- **Kalian**: 2nd person plural.
- **Saya**: Standard polite 1st person singular ("I").
- **Aku**: Intimate/informal 1st person singular.
- **Gue / Lu**: Informal Jakartan youth slang (*Gue* = I, *Lu* = you).

## 2. Epistolary Standards (Surat Resmi)
Formal administrative, corporate, and diplomatic letters in Indonesia follow rigid protocol:
- **Opening**:
  - *Dengan hormat,* (Standard formal salutation).
  - *Yang terhormat Bapak/Ibu [Nama/Jabatan],* (Abbreviated: *Yth. Bapak...*).
- **Opening Line**:
  - *Sehubungan dengan surat Saudara nomor...*
  - *Bersama ini kami sampaikan laporan teknis...*
- **Closing**:
  - *Demikian surat ini kami sampaikan. Atas perhatian dan kerja sama Bapak/Ibu, kami ucapkan terima kasih.*
  - *Hormat kami,* (Official corporate/institutional sign-off).
  - *Hormat saya,* (Individual formal sign-off).

## 3. Conversational Discourse Particles (Partikel Tutur)
Urban Indonesian features highly expressive pragmatic particles:
- **dong**: Persuasive insistence or claiming obviousness (*Bantu saya, dong!*).
- **sih**: Pragmatic softening or mild doubt/wonder (*Siapa sih dia?*).
- **kok**: Wonder or surprise at unexpected state (*Kok bisa begitu?*).
- **kan**: Tag question seeking agreement (*Dia sudah tahu, kan?*).
- **deh / nih**: Deictic emphasis (*Coba deh*, *Ini nih yang benar*).
