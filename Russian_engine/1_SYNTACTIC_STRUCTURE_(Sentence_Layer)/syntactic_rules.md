# Layer 1: Syntactic Structure (Sentence Layer) - Russian Engine (Русский язык)

## 1. Word Order Typology: Canonical SVO with Pragmatic Flexibility
Russian canonical neutral word order is **SVO (Subject - Verb - Object)**. However, because morphological case endings unambiguously identify syntactic arguments, Russian word order is pragmatically flexible for information packaging (Topic-Comment / Тема-Рема):

```
Canonical Neutral Clause Architecture:
[Subject (Nominative)] + [Finite Verb (Agreement)] + [Direct Object (Accusative)] + [Indirect Object (Dative)] + [Prepositional / Adverbial Phrase]
```

### Pragmatic Inversions:
- **SVO (Neutral)**: *Студент читает интересную книгу.* ("The student is reading an interesting book.")
- **OVS (Focus on Agent)**: *Эту книгу написал известный профессор.* ("A famous professor wrote this book.")
- **SOV (Literary / Contrastive Focus)**: *Он письмо другу отправил.* ("He sent the letter to a friend.")

---

## 2. Six-Case Syntactic Government (Шесть падежей)
Syntactic roles and prepositional government are mediated by six cases:

| Case | Primary Syntactic Role | Governing Prepositions | Exemplar |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nominative (Им.)** | Subject of finite clause | (None) | *Студент читает* |
| **Genitive (Род.)** | Possession, negation, quantifiers | *без, для, до, из, от, у, около, после* | *книга студента*, *нет времени* |
| **Dative (Дат.)** | Indirect object, experiencer | *к, по, благодаря, вопреки* | *сказать другу*, *мне холодно* |
| **Accusative (Вин.)** | Direct object, spatial goal | *в/на* (motion/direction), *через, за* | *вижу студента*, *иду в парк* |
| **Instrumental (Твор.)** | Instrument, agent, predicate nominal | *с, над, под, перед, за, между* | *писать ручкой*, *быть врачом* |
| **Prepositional (Предл.)** | Location, topic of thought/speech | *о/об, в/на* (static location), *при* | *думать о работе*, *жить в Москве* |

---

## 3. Animacy Split in the Accusative Case
Russian enforces a strict syntactic split in Accusative case endings based on semantic animacy:
1. **Masculine Second Declension**:
   - Inanimate: Accusative = Nominative (*Я вижу новый дом.*)
   - Animate: Accusative = Genitive (*Я вижу нового студента.*)
2. **Plural (All Genders)**:
   - Inanimate: Accusative = Nominative (*Я читаю новые книги / вижу новые столы.*)
   - Animate: Accusative = Genitive (*Я вижу новых студентов / красивых девушек.*)

---

## 4. Numeral Syntactic Concord (Синтаксис числительных)
Numeral government in the nominative case exhibits a tripartite agreement paradigm:
- **Ending in 1** (*один, двадцать один*): Governs **Nominative Singular** (*один стол, одна книга*).
- **Ending in 2, 3, 4** (*два, три, четыре, двадцать два*): Governs **Genitive Singular** (*два стола, три книги, четыре окна*).
- **Ending in 5 to 20, 0** (*пять, десять, двадцать, сто*): Governs **Genitive Plural** (*пять столов, десять книг, двадцать окон*).

---

## 5. Impersonal & Dative Experiencer Clauses
Subjectless constructions are widespread for physiological, psychological, and modal states:
- *Мне холодно.* ("I am cold" - lit. "To me cold").
- *Ему нужно работать.* ("He needs to work").
- *Нам кажется, что всё в порядке.* ("It seems to us that everything is fine").
- *В комнате нет места.* ("There is no space in the room" - existential negation with Genitive).
