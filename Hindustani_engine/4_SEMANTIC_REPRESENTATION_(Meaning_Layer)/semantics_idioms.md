# Hindustani Semantic Representation & Idiomatic Patterns (Meaning Layer)

## 1. Dative Subjects & Experiencer Constructions
In Hindustani, psychological states, physical ailments, obligations, and sensory perceptions are marked with **dative subject alignment** using the postposition **को (ko)**:
- **Desire / Need**: *Mujhe kitaab chahiye.* (To me book is needed = I want a book.)
- **Hunger / Thirst**: *Usko pyaas lagi hai.* (To him thirst is attached = He is thirsty.)
- **Obligation (Compulsion)**: *Humein jaana hai / padega.* (To us to go is / will fall = We have to go.)
- **Knowledge / Knowing**: *Aapko maloom hai.* (To you it is known = You know.)

In these constructions, the experiencer is the semantic subject, but the grammatical subject that triggers verbal agreement is the stimulus/object!

## 2. Inherent vs. Accidental Causation (Causative Derivation)
Hindustani verbs form a 3-tier causative morphological chain:
- **Base Intransitive / Transitive**: *kholna* (to open), *pakna* (to cook), *banna* (to be made).
- **First Causative (Direct agency - suffix -a-)**: *khilana* (to feed < *khaana*), *pakana* (to cook sth), *banana* (to make).
- **Second Causative (Indirect / mediated agency - suffix -wa-)**: *khilwana* (to have someone fed), *pakwana* (to have cooked), *banwana* (to have made).

## 3. High-Frequency Idioms & Proverbs
- *Daal mein kuch kaala hona* (दाल में कुछ काला होना): "Something fishy going on" (literal: something black in the lentils).
- *Aankhon ka taara* (आँखों का तारा): "Apple of one's eye" (literal: star of the eyes).
- *Haath malna* (हाथ मलना): "To regret deeply" (literal: to rub hands).
- *Billi ke bhaagon chheenka toota* (बिल्ली के भागों छींका टूटा): "An unexpected windfall by stroke of luck" (literal: the food net broke to the cat's fortune).
- *Naach na jaane aangan tedha* (नाच न जाने आँगन टेढ़ा): "A bad workman blames his tools" (literal: doesn't know how to dance, blames the courtyard for being crooked).
