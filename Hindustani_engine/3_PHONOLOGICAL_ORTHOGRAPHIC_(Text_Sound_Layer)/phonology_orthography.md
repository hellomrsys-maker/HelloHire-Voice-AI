# Hindustani Phonological & Orthographic Architecture (Text-Sound Layer)

## 1. Dual-Script Orthographic System
Hindustani represents a single pluricentric linguistic continuum recorded in two distinct standard scripts:
1. **Devanagari (देवनागरी)**:
   - Left-to-right alphasyllabary (abugida) used for Modern Standard Hindi.
   - Inherited inherent schwa (/ə/), virama (्) for consonant clustering, chandrabindu (ँ) and anusvara (ं) for vowel nasalization.
   - Nukta (़) diacritic to represent Perso-Arabic loan phonemes: क़ (/q/), ख़ (/x/), ग़ (/ɣ/), ज़ (/z/), फ़ (/f/).
2. **Perso-Arabic Nastaliq (نستعلیق)**:
   - Right-to-left cursive abjad used for Modern Standard Urdu.
   - Special retroflex diacritics (ط, small toey) on ٹ (/ʈ/), ڈ (/ɖ/), ڑ (/ɽ/).
   - Do-chashmi he (ھ) used for all aspirated consonants (پھ, تھ, جھ, etc.).

## 2. Consonantal Phonology
Hindustani possesses a 4-way contrast in oral stops (unvoiced unaspirated, unvoiced aspirated, voiced unaspirated, voiced aspirated) across five places of articulation:
- **Velar**: /k/ (क), /kʰ/ (ख), /g/ (ग), /gʱ/ (घ)
- **Palatal**: /tʃ/ (च), /tʃʰ/ (छ), /dʒ/ (ज), /dʒʱ/ (झ)
- **Retroflex**: /ʈ/ (ट), /ʈʰ/ (ठ), /ɖ/ (ड), /ɖʱ/ (ढ), /ɽ/ (ड़), /ɽʱ/ (ढ़)
- **Dental**: /t̪/ (त), /t̪ʰ/ (थ), /d̪/ (द), /d̪ʱ/ (ध)
- **Labial**: /p/ (प), /pʰ/ (फ), /b/ (ब), /bʱ/ (भ)

### Retroflex Flap (/ɽ/, /ɽʱ/):
A defining phonological signature of South Asian languages. Never occurs in word-initial position in native vocabulary (*bada, padhna, gaadi*).

## 3. Vowel Inventory & Nasalization
- **Short vowels**: /ə/ (अ), /ɪ/ (इ), /ʊ/ (उ)
- **Long vowels**: /aː/ (आ), /iː/ (ई), /uː/ (ऊ), /eː/ (ए), /ɛː/ (ऐ), /oː/ (ओ), /ɔː/ (औ)
- **Phonemic Nasalization**: Every oral vowel has an exact nasal counterpart (/ãː/, /ĩː/, /ũː/, etc.). Contrast between oral and nasal vowels is lexically contrastive:
  - *hai* (है - is, singular) vs. *hain* (हैं - are, plural/honorific).
  - *saas* (सास - mother-in-law) vs. *saans* (साँस - breath).
