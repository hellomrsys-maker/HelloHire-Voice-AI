# World Hindustani Dialects & Diachronic Evolution (Variation Layer)

## 1. Dialectal & Regional Variations
1. **Khariboli (Standard Delhi / Western UP Base)**:
   - Historical foundation for both Modern Standard Hindi and Modern Standard Urdu.
   - Characterized by distinct geminates and strong retroflex consonants.
2. **Bambaiya Hindi / Mumbai Tapori**:
   - High-energy urban dialect blending Marathi, Gujarati, and English lexical elements.
   - Uses *apna* as generalized first person, invariant tags (*kya bolta hai*, *khallas*).
3. **Dakhini / Hyderabadi Urdu**:
   - Historical southern variety preserved in the Deccan (Hyderabad, Aurangabad).
   - Retains archaic features: *nakko* (negative imperative "don't!"), *hau* (yes), distinctive progressive aspect *-re* (*kare so hai*).
4. **Bhojpuri / Awadhi Influenced Eastern Hindi**:
   - Use of *hum* as 1st person singular instead of *main*.
   - Ergative *ne* is often absent or dropped in colloquial spoken discourse.
5. **Diasporic Varieties**:
   - **Fiji Hindi**: Distinctive ko-marking and simplified verbal inflections.
   - **Caribbean Hindustani (Surinamese Sarnami / Trinidadian)**: Preserves Eastern Bhojpuri elements.

## 2. Diachronic Evolution
- **Sanskrit / Prakrit / Apabhramsha (Ancient - 10th c.)**: Loss of dual number, reduction of 8-case synthetic declensions to 2-case analytical system (Direct vs Oblique) supported by postpositions.
- **Old Hindustani / Hindavi (12th - 17th c.)**: Amir Khusrau's riddles and Kabir's poetry establishing the mixed Indo-Persian register.
- **Reekhta / Urdu-e-Mualla (18th - 19th c.)**: Courtly refinement in Delhi and Lucknow under Ghalib and Mir.
- **Modern Bifurcation (20th c. - Present)**: Division into Modern Standard Hindi (Sanskritized vocabulary) and Modern Standard Urdu (Persianized vocabulary), united by a single identical colloquial core grammar.
