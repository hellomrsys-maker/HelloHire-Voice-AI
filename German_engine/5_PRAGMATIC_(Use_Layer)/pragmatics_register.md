# German Engine — Layer 5: Pragmatic Representation (Use Layer)

## 1. The German T-V Distinction: Duzen vs. Siezen

German maintains a rigorous, socially significant T-V (Tu-Vos) distinction that governs social hierarchy, intimacy, workplace relations, and institutional protocol:

### 1.1 Pronoun Matrix & Capitalization
| Pronoun Type | Informal Singular (Duzen) | Informal Plural (Ihrzen) | Formal (Siezen - Sing. & Plur.) |
|--------------|---------------------------|--------------------------|---------------------------------|
| Nominativ    | *du*                      | *ihr*                    | *Sie* (capitalized)             |
| Akkusativ    | *dich*                    | *euch*                   | *Sie* (capitalized)             |
| Dativ        | *dir*                     | *euch*                   | *Ihnen* (capitalized)           |
| Genitiv / Possessiv | *dein / deine / deinem* | *euer / eure / eurem*   | *Ihr / Ihre / Ihrem* (capitalized) |

> **Critical Rule on Orthography**: The formal pronoun *Sie*, *Ihnen*, and possessive *Ihr/Ihre* MUST ALWAYS be capitalized in all contexts to distinguish them from 3rd person plural (*sie/ihnen/ihr* - "they/them/their") and 3rd person singular feminine (*sie/ihr* - "she/her"). In informal letters, *Du/Dir/Dein* may optionally be capitalized, but modern Duden recommends lower-case *du/dir/dein*.

### 1.2 Social Domains and Transitions
- **Familial & Intimate**: *Duzen* is universal among family members, close friends, students/pupils among each other, and children under ~15.
- **Professional & Public**: *Siezen* is the default between adults who do not know each other, in customer service, legal proceedings, public administration, and traditional corporate hierarchy.
- **The "Hamburger Sie"**: Combination of first name with formal *Sie* (*"Peter, haben Sie den Bericht fertig?"*), common in multinational tech offices.
- **The "Münchner Du"**: Combination of last name with informal *du* (*"Frau Müller, hast du kurz Zeit?"*), rare and regional.
- **Das Angebot des Du**: Transition from *Sie* to *du* is governed by strict etiquette: the socially senior or older person (or hierarchically superior in business) must explicitly offer the *du* (*"Wir können uns gerne duzen"*).

## 2. Honorifics and Address Protocol

German address forms combine academic titles, professional roles, and marital/gender status:

- **Formal Salutation**:
  - Male: *Herr* + [Academic Title] + [Last Name] (e.g., *Herr Dr. Weber*, *Herr Professor Schmidt*).
  - Female: *Frau* + [Academic Title] + [Last Name] (e.g., *Frau Dr. Weber*, *Frau Professorin Schmidt*).
  - Note on *Fräulein*: Obsolete and socially unacceptable since the 1970s; all adult females are addressed as *Frau*.
- **Multiple Academic Titles**: Ranked hierarchically (*Herr Prof. Dr. med. Dr. h.c. Lehmann*). In spoken address, usually only the highest title is used (*"Guten Tag, Herr Professor"*).

## 3. Epistolary Formulas (Brief- und E-Mail-Konventionen)

German epistolary style follows rigid conventions of salutation, punctuation, case capitalization, and complimentary close:

### 3.1 Salutation Schemes
1. **Ultra-Formal / Impersonal**:
   - *Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren,*
   - (Next line starts lowercase unless it begins with a capitalized noun).
2. **Formal / Named**:
   - Male: *Sehr geehrter Herr Dr. Meyer,*
   - Female: *Sehr geehrte Frau Müller,*
3. **Semi-Formal / Professional Colleague**:
   - *Liebe Kolleginnen und Kollegen,*
   - *Guten Tag, Frau Schmidt,*
4. **Informal / Personal**:
   - Male: *Lieber Thomas,*
   - Female: *Liebe Claudia,*
   - Plural: *Liebe Freunde, / Hallo zusammen,*

### 3.2 Complimentary Closes
- **Formal**: *Mit freundlichen Grüßen* (No comma following the close in German orthography!).
- **Slightly Less Formal**: *Mit besten Grüßen*, *Freundliche Grüße*.
- **Semi-Formal**: *Beste Grüße aus Berlin*, *Viele Grüße*.
- **Informal**: *Herzliche Grüße*, *Liebe Grüße*, *Bis bald*, *Dein / Deine [Name]*.

## 4. Modal Particles (Abtönungspartikeln) and Evidentiality

Modal particles do not alter the propositional truth value of an utterance, but encode the speaker's pragmatic stance, mutual assumptions, emotional tone, and epistemic commitment:

| Particle | Pragmatic Function | Example Sentence | English Pragmatic Gloss |
|----------|-------------------|------------------|-------------------------|
| *ja*     | Common knowledge / shared assumption | *Das weißt du ja.* | "As you well know / obviously." |
| *doch*   | Reminder against counter-belief / mild rebuke | *Komm doch endlich!* | "Do come on! / Why don't you come?" |
| *mal*    | Softener / minimization of burden in requests | *Schau mal hier.* | "Take a quick look here." |
| *denn*   | Expresses genuine interest or skepticism in questions | *Wo bist du denn gewesen?* | "Where on earth have you been?" |
| *eben / halt* | Resignation / inevitable fact / acceptance | *Das ist eben so.* | "That's just the way it is." |
| *wohl*   | Epistemic conjecture / probability | *Er wird wohl krank sein.* | "He is probably sick." |
| *schon*  | Concessive reassurance or impatience | *Das wird schon klappen.* | "It will work out, don't worry." |
| *bloß / nur* | Urgency or warning in imperatives/questions | *Mach das bloß nicht!* | "Whatever you do, don't do that!" |

## 5. Register Continuum & Stylistic Varieties

```
[Alltagssprache / Umgangssprache] <---> [Standardsprache] <---> [Fach- / Bildungs- / Beamtendeutsch]
```

### 5.1 Kanzleideutsch / Beamtendeutsch (Administrative/Legal Register)
- **Nominalstil (Nominal Style)**: Heavy preference for deverbal nouns instead of finite verbs:
  - *Verbal*: *Weil das Gesetz geändert wurde, müssen wir handeln.*
  - *Nominalstil*: *Aufgrund der Gesetzesänderung besteht Handlungsbedarf.*
- **Passive Constructions & Impersonal Stance**:
  - *Es wird darauf hingewiesen, dass...* (Impersonal passive).
  - *Zuständige Behörde*, *ordnungsgemäße Einreichung*, *Antragstellung*.
- **Partizipialkonstruktionen (Extended participial attributes)**:
  - *Die vom Bundesministerium für Wirtschaft vorgelegte Verordnung...*

### 5.2 Bildungsbürgerlicher Register (Literary / Scholarly)
- Frequent use of **Konjunktiv I** for indirect discourse (*Der Minister betonte, man müsse die Reformen vorantreiben*).
- Genitive constructions where vernacular uses dative (*Wegen des Regens* vs. spoken *Wegen dem Regen*).
- Präteritum rather than Perfekt for past narrative.
