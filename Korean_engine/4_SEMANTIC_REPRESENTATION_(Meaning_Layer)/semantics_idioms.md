# Korean Engine — Layer 4: Semantic Representation (Meaning Layer)

## 1. Lexical Honorifics vs. Plain Counterparts

Korean maintains a rich inventory of supplanted lexical honorifics (*특수 어휘*) that replace basic plain terms when speaking of or addressing social superiors:

### 1.1 Nominals (Nouns & Titles)
| Plain Form (*평칭*) | Honorific Form (*존칭*) | English Gloss | Concord Constraint |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *밥* | *진지* | meal / cooked rice | Used with elders/superiors: *진지 잡수셨어요?* |
| *집* | *댁* | residence / house | *선생님 댁* (teacher's residence) |
| *나이* | *연세* | age | *할아버지 연세* (grandfather's age) |
| *생일* | *생신* | birthday | *어머니 생신* (mother's birthday) |
| *말* | *말씀* | speech / words | Self-humbling or other-exalting |
| *사람* | *분* | person / individual | Counter: *두 분* (two respected persons) |
| *이름* | *성함* | name | *성함이 어떻게 되세요?* |

### 1.2 Predicates (Verbs & Adjectives)
| Plain Verb (*평칭*) | Honorific Verb (*존칭*) | English Gloss | Special Semantic Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *자다* | *주무시다* | to sleep | Replaces *자시다* |
| *먹다 / 마시다* | *드시다 / 잡수시다* | to eat / drink | *드시다* is most frequent |
| *있다* (Subject presence) | *계시다* | to be / stay | Subject is superior: *선생님께서 교실에 계신다.* |
| *있다* (Attribute possession) | *있으시다* | to have / possess | Attribute of superior: *선생님은 차가 있으시다.* |
| *죽다* | *돌아가시다* | to pass away | Euphemistic honorific |
| *아프다* | *편찮으시다* | to be unwell / sick | *할머니께서 편찮으시다.* |
| *주다* (to equal/inferior) | *드리다* (to superior) | to give | Humilific: *선생님께 선물을 드렸다.* |
| *데리다* | *모시다* | to accompany / escort | *부모님을 모시고 가다.* |
| *묻다* | *여쭙다 / 여쭤보다* | to inquire / ask | Humilific question to superior |
| *만나다 / 보다* | *뵙다 / 찾아뵙다* | to meet / see | Humilific encounter with superior |

## 2. Ideophones: Sound and Manner Symbolism (의성어 / 의태어)

Korean is internationally famous for its pervasive sound-symbolic vocabulary, governed by ancient vowel harmony contrasts:
- **Light / Bright Vowels (*양성모음*: `ㅏ, ㅗ`)**: Smallness, brightness, lightness, cheerfulness.
- **Dark / Heavy Vowels (*음성모음*: `ㅓ, ㅜ`)**: Largeness, darkness, heaviness, seriousness.

| Bright / Diminutive Form | Dark / Augmentative Form | Semantic Contrast |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *반짝반짝* | *번쩍번쩍* | bright twinkling (stars/eyes) vs. blinding flash (lightning) |
| *살금살금* | *슬금슬금* | light stealthy tiptoeing vs. heavier sneaky crawling |
| *알록달록* | *얼룩덜룩* | brightly multi-colored pattern vs. stained blotchy dark marks |
| *졸졸* | *줄줄* | gentle pleasant trickling vs. heavy gushing flow |
| *퐁당퐁당* | *풍덩풍덩* | small pebble dropping into pond vs. heavy splash |

## 3. Four-Character Sino-Korean Idioms (사자성어 / 四字成語)

Sino-Korean quadri-character idioms condense deep philosophical and pragmatic observations:
- **고진감래 (苦盡甘來)**: "Bitterness ends, sweetness arrives" — Reward comes after hard labor.
- **일석이조 (一石二鳥)**: "One stone, two birds" — Killing two birds with one stone.
- **작심삼일 (作心三日)**: "A resolution lasts only three days" — Short-lived determination.
- **동문서답 (東問西答)**: "Asking east, answering west" — Giving an irrelevant answer.
- **우이독경 (牛耳讀經)**: "Reading scriptures into an ox's ear" — Talking to someone who refuses to listen.
- **금상첨화 (錦上添花)**: "Adding flowers to fine brocade" — An already wonderful thing made even better.

## 4. Auxiliary Predicate Semantics (보조 용언 체계)

1. **`-아/어 보다` (Experiential / Trial)**:
   - *김치를 먹어 봤어요.* ("I have tried eating kimchi.")
2. **`-아/어 버리다` (Completive / Finality with Relief or Regret)**:
   - *기차가 떠나 버렸다.* ("The train has left [to my great regret].")
3. **`-아/어 두다 / 놓다` (Preparatory Preservation)**:
   - *표를 미리 사 두었다.* ("Bought the tickets in advance [for later use].")
4. **`-아/어 주다 / 드리다` (Benefactive Transfer)**:
   - *도와 드릴게요.* ("I will assist you [honoring you as recipient].")
