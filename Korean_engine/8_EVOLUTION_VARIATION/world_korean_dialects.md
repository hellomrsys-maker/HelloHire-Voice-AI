# Korean Engine — Layer 8: Evolution, Variation & Dialectology

## 1. Historical Stages of the Korean Language

### 1.1 Old Korean (고대 한국어, to 10th Century)
- Spoken during the Three Kingdoms (Goguryeo, Baekje, Silla) and Unified Silla period.
- Transcribed using Chinese characters via indigenous orthographic systems:
  - **Hyangchal (향찰)**: System for transcribing vernacular Korean poetry (*Hyangga*) where Chinese characters represented either Korean morpheme roots (logographically) or grammatical inflections (phonetically).
  - **Idu (이두) & Gugyeol (구결)**: Administrative glossing systems used by clerks.

### 1.2 Middle Korean (중세 한국어, 10th–16th Century) & The 1446 Invention
- In 1446, King Sejong the Great published the **Hunminjeongeum (훈민정음 / "Correct Sounds to Instruct the People")**, creating the Hangul alphabet.
- Characteristics:
  - **Tone / Pitch Accents**: Marked with side dots (*방점*): no dot (평성 / low), one dot (거성 / high), two dots (상성 / rising).
  - **Extinct Graphemes**: *ㆍ* (아래아 / Arae-a), *ㅿ* (반시옷 / voiced alveolar fricative [z]), *ㆁ* (옛이응 / velar nasal [ŋ]), *ㅸ* (순경음 비읍 / bilabial fricative [β]).
  - **Strict Vowel Harmony**: Division into Bright (*양성*: `ㆍ, ㅏ, ㅗ`) and Dark (*음성*: `ㅡ, ㅓ, ㅜ`) vowels.

### 1.3 Early Modern & Modern Standardization
- 1933: The Korean Language Society enacted the *Unified Hangul Orthography (한글 맞춤법 통일안)*, formalizing morphophonemic spelling.

## 2. North-South Pluricentric Divergence (남북 언어 차이)

Following the 1945 division of Korea, standard varieties developed under different political regimes:

### 2.1 Pyojun-eo (표준어 - South Korea) vs. Munhwa-eo (문화어 - North Korea)
| Linguistic Feature | South Korean (*표준어*) | North Korean (*문화어*) | Linguistic Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Initial Sound Rule (*두음법칙*)** | **Enforced**: Initial `ㄹ` and `ㄴ` shift/drop: *노동, 이성, 여자* | **Abolished**: Historical initials preserved: *로동, 리성, 녀자* | South avoids initial liquids/nasals before high vowels; North maintains etymological Hanja integrity |
| **Spacing Rules (*띄어쓰기*)** | More spaces (words/dependent nouns separated): *할 수 있다* | Fewer spaces (agglutinated compounds): *할수있다* | North emphasizes morphological bonding in compound phrases |
| **Foreign Loanwords** | Heavy borrowing from English: *아이스크림, 주차장, 도넛* | Systematic nativization / Russian influence: *얼음보숭이, 차세우개, 가락지빵* | Linguistic purism policy in North Korea (*말다듬기 운동*) |

## 3. Major Regional Dialect Groups (한국의 방언 체계)

```
                            Korean Dialect Map
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ Hamgyong & Pyongan (Northern Dialects)                      │
   │  - Strict preservation of initial consonants, retroflexion  │
   ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ Central Dialect (Seoul, Gyeonggi, Gangwon Yeongseo)         │
   │  - Non-tonal, basis for standard Pyojun-eo                  │
   ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ Chungcheong Dialect                                         │
   │  - Relaxed syllable elongation, ending '-유 / -유~'         │
   ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ Jeolla Dialect (Southwestern / Honam)                       │
   │  - Rich aspectual particles (-부러), discourse markers (-잉)│
   ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ Gyeongsang Dialect (Southeastern / Yeongnam)                │
   │  - True tonal pitch accent, interrogative '-노 / -가' split │
   ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ Jeju Language (제주어)                                      │
   │  - Retains ancient Arae-a (ㆍ), mutually unintelligible     │
   └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The Korean Engine incorporates awareness of South Korean standard conventions (including *두음법칙*), while providing morphological normalization for dialectal and North Korean variations.
