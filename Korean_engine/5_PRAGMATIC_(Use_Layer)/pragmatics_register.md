# Korean Engine — Layer 5: Pragmatic Representation (Use Layer)

## 1. The Korean Speech Level System (상대높임법)

Korean possesses one of the most sophisticated speech level hierarchies in world linguistics. Every finite predicate obligatorily selects a sentence-final ending (*종결어미*) encoding the speaker-hearer relationship, social distance, hierarchy, and situational formality:

### 1.1 The Seven Traditional Speech Levels
| Speech Level | Formality | Politeness | Declarative Ending | Interrogative Ending | Imperative Ending | Sociolinguistic Domain |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **하십시오체 (Hasipsio-che)** | Formal | High Deferential | `-습니다 / -ㅂ니다` | `-습니까 / -ㅂ니까` | `-하십시오 / -(으)십시오` | News broadcasts, military, business presentations, public service, first-time formal meetings |
| **해요체 (Haeyo-che)** | Informal | Polite | `-아요 / -어요` | `-아요 / -어요?` | `-아/어요 / -(으)세요` | Default everyday standard between adults, polite workplace conversation, friendly public discourse |
| **하게체 (Hage-che)** | Formal | Neutral/Moderate | `-네` | `-나 / -는가` | `-게` | Older professors/elders addressing adult juniors or sons-in-law |
| **하오체 (Hao-che)** | Formal | Moderate | `-오 / -소` | `-오 / -소` | `-구려 / -오` | Historical drama, older authority speaking to equals; archaic in modern spoken Korean |
| **해라체 (Haera-che)** | Formal/Written | Plain/Non-Honorific | `-(느)ㄴ다 / -다` | `-니 / -냐` | `-아라 / -어라` | Books, newspapers, academic essays, close peers of same age, adults to children |
| **해체 (Hae-che)** | Informal | Intimate (반말) | `-아 / -어` | `-아 / -어?` | `-아 / -어` | Close friends, younger siblings, intimate partners |

> **Modern Reality**: In contemporary 21st-century spoken Korean, the system effectively concentrates into **하십시오체** (formal deferential), **해요체** (polite informal), **해라체** (written plain), and **해체** (intimate *반말*).

## 2. Tripartite Honorification Architecture

Korean honorification operates across three orthogonal syntactic axes:

```
                          Korean Honorification
                                   │
       ┌───────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┐
       ▼                           ▼                           ▼
[ 주체높임법 ]                [ 객체높임법 ]              [ 상대높임법 ]
Subject Honorification       Object Honorification       Addressee Honorification
- Focus: Sentential Subject  - Focus: Object/Dative      - Focus: Interlocutor/Listener
- Infix: -(으)시-            - Lexical: 드리다, 모시다   - Endings: -습니다, -어요, etc.
- Particle: -께서             - Particle: -께             - Pervasive across all clauses
```

### 2.1 Subject Honorification (주체높임법)
Elevates the grammatical subject of the sentence:
- Regular: Insert pre-final suffix `-(으)시-`:
  - *가다* $\to$ *가시다* $\to$ *가십니다 / 가세요*
  - *읽다* $\to$ *읽으시다* $\to$ *읽으십니다 / 읽으세요*
- Particle Concord: Replace Subject particle `이/가` with `께서`:
  - *선생님께서 오셨습니다.* ("The teacher has arrived.")
- Lexical Replacements:
  - *주무시다* (sleep), *드시다* (eat), *계시다* (stay), *돌아가시다* (pass away).

### 2.2 Object / Addressee-Beneficiary Honorification (객체높임법)
Lowers the speaker or subject relative to a respected indirect/direct object:
- Replace `에게/한테` with `께`:
  - *할머니께 편지를 드렸습니다.* ("Gave a letter to grandmother.")
- Lexical Suppletions: *드리다* (give), *모시다* (escort), *여쭙다* (ask), *뵙다* (meet).

## 3. Social Deixis, Kinship & Professional Titles

In Korean social communication, using bare second-person pronouns (*당신, 너*) to social superiors or unfamiliar adults is a severe pragmatic violation:
- **Kinship Terms as Pronouns**:
  - *형* (older brother for males), *누나* (older sister for males)
  - *오빠* (older brother for females), *언니* (older sister for females)
- **Honorific Title Suffix `-님`**:
  - Mandatory in professional hierarchy: *선생님* (teacher), *교수님* (professor), *팀장님* (team lead), *과장님* (manager), *부장님* (director), *대표님 / 사장님* (CEO/president).
  - Calling a manager by last name without title (*"김철수!"*) is strictly taboo.

## 4. Epistolary Formulas (이메일 및 서신 규범)

### 4.1 Salutations & Openings
- Formal Business Opening:
  - *안녕하십니까, [회사명/부서명]의 [이름] [직급]입니다.* ("Hello, I am [Name] [Title] from [Company/Dept].")
  - *존경하는 [이름] [직급]님께,* ("To respected [Name] [Title],")
- Seasonal & Well-being Opening:
  - *환절기에 건강 유의하시기 바랍니다.* ("Please take care of your health during this seasonal change.")

### 4.2 Complimentary Closings
- Standard Formal Business Closing:
  - *감사합니다. [보내는 사람] 올림 / 드림.* ("Thank you. Respectfully presented by [Sender].")
  - *좋은 하루 보내십시오.* ("Have a good day.")
