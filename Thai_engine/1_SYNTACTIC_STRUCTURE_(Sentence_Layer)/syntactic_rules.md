# Syntactic Structure & Grammar Rules for Thai (Sentence Layer)
## Sovereign Engine: `Thai_engine` | Tai-Kadai Anchor

### 1. Canonical Isolating SVO Word Order
Thai is an isolating / analytic language characterized by canonical **SVO (Subject - Verb - Object)** topology with head-initial noun phrases:

$$\text{Clause} \to (\text{Subject}) + (\text{Preverbal Aux/Aspect}) + \text{Verb} + (\text{Object}) + (\text{Adverbial}) + (\text{Politeness Particle})$$

#### Examples:
1. **Canonical SVO**:
   *ผมกินข้าว* (*phǒm kin khâaw*)
   `I (male) + eat + rice` -> "I eat rice."
2. **Serial Verb Construction (SVC)**:
   *เขาเดินไปซื้อของที่ตลาด* (*khǎw doən pay sɯ́ɯ khɔ̌ɔŋ thîi talàat*)
   `He walk go buy things at market` -> "He walked to the market to buy things."

### 2. Head-Initial Noun Phrase Structure & Classifiers
In Thai, modifiers strictly follow the head noun. When quantifying nouns, Thai mandatorily uses the post-nominal numeral classifier formula:

$$\text{Noun Phrase} = \text{Head Noun} + (\text{Adjective}) + \text{Numeral} + \text{Classifier} + (\text{Demonstrative})$$

#### Classifier Syntax Formulas:
1. **Quantified Noun**: `[Noun] + [Numeral] + [Classifier]`
   - *หมาสองตัว* (*mǎa sɔ̌ɔŋ tua*): "dog two [body/animal-CLF]" = two dogs.
   - *หนังสือสามเล่ม* (*nǎŋsɯ̌ɯ sǎam lêm*): "book three [volume-CLF]" = three books.
2. **Demonstrative Noun**: `[Noun] + [Classifier] + [Demonstrative]`
   - *หมาตัวนี้* (*mǎa tua níi*): "dog [CLF] this" = this dog.
   - *หนังสือเล่มนั้น* (*nǎŋsɯ̌ɯ lêm nán*): "book [CLF] that" = that book.
3. **Indefinite ("One") Noun**: `[Noun] + [Classifier] + หนึ่ง (nɯ̀ŋ)`
   - *คนหนึ่ง* (*khon nɯ̀ŋ*): "person [CLF] one" = a person.

*Pre-nominal placement (e.g. \*สองตัวหมา) is an ungrammatical foreign calque.*

### 3. Tense, Aspect, and Modality (TAM) Auxiliaries
Tense and aspect are expressed via preverbal and postverbal aspect markers, with zero verbal inflection:

| Marker | Position | Aspect / Modality | Example | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **กำลัง** (*kamlaŋ*) | Preverbal | Continuous / Progressive | *เขากำลังอ่านหนังสือ* | "He is reading a book" |
| **ได้** (*dâay*) | Preverbal | Past / Achievement | *เขาได้ไปเชียงใหม่* | "He went to Chiang Mai" |
| **จะ** (*cà*) | Preverbal | Future / Irrealis | *พรุ่งนี้ฉันจะไป* | "Tomorrow I will go" |
| **แล้ว** (*lɛ́ɛw*) | Postverbal / Final | Perfective / Completed | *กินข้าวแล้ว* | "Already ate rice" |
| **เคย** (*khəəy*) | Preverbal | Experiential ("have ever") | *เคยไปเมืองไทย* | "Have been to Thailand" |
| **อยู่** (*yùu*) | Postverbal | Durative ongoing state | *นอนอยู่* | "Is sleeping" |

### 4. Negation Syntax: *ไม่* (*mây*) vs *ไม่ได้* (*mây dâay*)
- **ไม่** (*mây*): Standard declarative preverbal negator ("do not / does not"):
  - *เขาไม่กินเนื้อ* (*khǎw mây kin nɯ́a* - "He does not eat meat.")
- **ไม่ได้** (*mây dâay*): Past / achievement negator ("did not / failed to"):
  - *เมื่อวานไม่ได้ไป* (*mɯ̂a-waan mây dâay pay* - "Yesterday did not go.")
- **อย่า** (*yàa*): Prohibitive negative imperative ("do not!"):
  - *อย่าทำ* (*yàa tham* - "Don't do it!")
