# Swahili Engine — Layer 4: Semantic Representation (Meaning Layer)

## 1. Proverbial Wisdom & Metaphorical Architecture (Methali)

In Swahili culture and discourse, proverbs (*Methali*) are not merely rhetorical flourishes; they carry legal, ethical, and communicative authority:

### 1.1 Core Canonical Proverbs
| Swahili Proverb | Literal Translation | Pragmatic / Metaphorical Meaning | Discourse Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Haraka haraka haina baraka** | Hurry, hurry has no blessing | Haste makes waste; impatience brings ruin | Counsel against rushed decisions |
| **Pole pole ndio mwendo** | Slowly, slowly is the real movement | Slow and steady wins the race | Encouragement during long tasks |
| **Akili ni mali** | Intelligence is wealth | Knowledge and wisdom are the truest riches | Value of education and thought |
| **Asiyesikia la mkuu huvunjika guu** | He who does not listen to an elder breaks his leg | Disregarding wise advice leads to injury | Moral guidance to youth |
| **Mtaka cha mvunguni sharti ainame** | He who desires what is under the bed must bend down | Success requires humility and effort | Motivating hard work |
| **Haba na haba hujaza kibaba** | Little and little fills the measure | Drops make an ocean; incremental progress adds up | Thrift, savings, and perseverance |
| **Umoja ni nguvu, utengano ni udhaifu** | Unity is strength, division is weakness | United we stand, divided we fall | Community solidarity |
| **Kikulacho ki nguoni mwako** | That which eats you is inside your clothes | Your worst enemy is often someone closest to you | Caution regarding betrayal |

## 2. Idiomatic Verbal Expressions (Nahau)

Swahili idioms (*Nahau*) frequently pair functional light verbs (*kupiga* [to strike], *kula* [to eat], *kukata* [to cut], *kutia* [to put]) with semantic complements:

### 2.1 Common Nahau Inventory
- **Kupiga mbizi**: lit. "to strike a dive" $\to$ to dive deeply / investigate thoroughly.
- **Kula njama**: lit. "to eat conspiracy" $\to$ to plot or conspire.
- **Kukata tamaa**: lit. "to cut hope" $\to$ to give up / lose hope completely.
- **Kutia moyo**: lit. "to put heart" $\to$ to encourage / embolden someone.
- **Kuvunja moyo**: lit. "to break heart" $\to$ to discourage / demoralize.
- **Kufunga safari**: lit. "to bind a journey" $\to$ to set out on an expedition.
- **Kupiga picha**: lit. "to strike picture" $\to$ to take a photograph.
- **Kupiga chafya**: lit. "to strike sneeze" $\to$ to sneeze.

## 3. Reduplication & Semantic Intensification (Urudiaji)

Productive reduplication in Swahili conveys distribution, attenuation, or high intensity:
- **polepole**: "slowly, gently, carefully" (from *pole* [sorry/sympathy]).
- **sawasawa**: "just right, exact, equal" (from *sawa* [equal/okay]).
- **mbalimbali**: "various, distinct, diverse" (from *mbali* [far/away]).
- **kidogokidogo**: "little by little, gradually" (from *kidogo* [small/little]).
- **mara kwa mara**: "from time to time, repeatedly" (from *mara* [time/instance]).

## 4. Locative Semantics & The *-Ni* Suffix

Attaching the locative enclitic `-ni` shifts a concrete noun into the locative class system (Classes 16, 17, 18):
- *nyumba* (house) $\to$ *nyumbani* (at/in/to the house).
- *shule* (school) $\to$ *shuleni* (at school).
- *mto* (river) $\to$ *mtoni* (at/in the river).
- *kitabu* (book) $\to$ *kitabuni* (in the book).

> **Crucial Syntactic Constraint**: When `-ni` is added to a noun, it cannot take regular class adjectives or demonstratives; it obligatorily triggers **locative concord** (Classes 16, 17, or 18):
> - *Nyumba hii* (This house [Class 9])
> - *Nyumbani **humu*** (Inside this house [Class 18 - *mu-*])
> - *Nyumbani **hapa*** (Here at the house [Class 16 - *pa-*])
