## 1. The Swahili Greeting System (Mfumo wa Salamu)

In Swahili society and communicative etiquette, greetings are mandatory prerequisites before any transactional or informative discourse can begin. Skipping a greeting is considered offensive:

### 1.1 The Intergenerational Respect Greeting: Shikamoo & Marahaba
- **Shikamoo**: Originating from the historical phrase *Nashika miguu yako* ("I hold your feet"). Obligatorily uttered by younger people, students, or juniors to elders, parents, teachers, and superiors.
- **Marahaba**: The obligatory asymmetric response from the elder, meaning "I accept your respect with delight."
- **Pragmatic Rule**: An elder NEVER says *Shikamoo* to a child, and a child NEVER replies with *Marahaba*.

### 1.2 Symmetric State of Being: Hujambo & Sijambo
- **Singular**:
  - Speaker A: *Hujambo?* (lit. "Do you have no trouble/matter?")
  - Speaker B: *Sijambo.* ("I have no trouble.")
- **Plural**:
  - Speaker A: *Hamjambo?* ("Do you all have no trouble?")
  - Speaker B: *Hatujambo.* ("We have no trouble.")

### 1.3 News Inquiries: Habari Gani?
- *Habari za asubuhi?* (Morning news?) $\to$ *Nzuri / Salama / Njema*.
- *Habari za mchana?* (Afternoon news?) $\to$ *Nzuri / Salama*.
- *Habari za jioni?* (Evening news?) $\to$ *Nzuri / Salama*.
- *Habari za kazi?* (How is work?) $\to$ *Nzuri sana*.

## 2. Politeness Markers & Social Etiquette

| Politeness Marker | Meaning | Sociolinguistic Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tafadhali** | Please | Softening requests and instructions |
| **Asante / Asante sana** | Thank you / Thank you very much | Expressing gratitude (Plural: *Asanteni*) |
| **Karibu / Karibuni** | Welcome / Make yourself at home | Welcoming guests or responding to *Asante* |
| **Samahani** | Excuse me / I am sorry / Pardon | Apology or politely getting attention |
| **Pole / Pole sana** | Sympathy / I am sorry for your hardship| Conveying empathy for illness, fatigue, or accident |

## 3. Social Deixis & Honorific Titles

1. **Mzee** (pl. *Wazee*): Respected elder. Carries immense cultural authority; used as a title of supreme reverence.
2. **Bwana / Bibi**:
   - *Bwana*: Mr. / Sir (e.g. *Bwana Juma*).
   - *Bibi*: Mrs. / Madam / Grandmother (e.g. *Bibi Mwajuma*).
3. **Mheshimiwa** (pl. *Waheshimiwa*): "Honorable / His/Her Excellency". Obligatory for members of parliament, judges, cabinet ministers, and heads of state.
4. **Ndugu**: "Brother / Comrade / Citizen". Widely used in formal Tanzanian civic discourse to express equality and solidarity.
5. **Kaka / Dada**: Older brother / Older sister; common respectful term for peers.

## 4. Epistolary Standards (Uandishi wa Barua na Barua Pepe)

### 4.1 Formal Business Letter Openings
- *Kwa Mheshimiwa [Jina],* ("To the Honorable [Name],")
- *Kwa Bwana / Bibi [Jina],* ("To Mr. / Mrs. [Name],")
- *Ndugu Mkurugenzi,* ("Comrade / Respected Director,")
- Opening formula: *Natumai barua hii inakukuta ukiwa mzima wa afya.* ("I hope this letter finds you in good health.")

### 4.2 Complimentary Closings
- **Formal / Official**:
  - *Wako mtiifu,* ("Yours obediently,")
  - *Wako mwaminifu,* ("Yours faithfully,")
  - *Wako katika ujenzi wa taifa,* ("Yours in the building of the nation," - civic formal)
- **Semi-Formal / Professional**:
  - *Wako katika kazi,* ("Yours in work,")
  - *Wasalaam,* ("With peace / Best regards,")
- **Informal / Personal**:
  - *Wako akupendaye,* ("Yours who loves you,")
  - *Ni mimi rafiki yako,* ("It is I, your friend,")
