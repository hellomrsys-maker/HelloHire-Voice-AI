# Layer 1: Syntactic Structure (Sentence Layer) — Spanish Engine

## 1. Canonical Word Order & Pro-Drop Typology
- **Basic Word Order**: Canonical **SVO** (*Sujeto - Verbo - Objeto*).
  - *El profesor explica la lección.*
- **Pro-Drop (Null-Subject Language)**: Because Spanish verbal inflection encodes person and number with high morphological specificity, overt subject pronouns are typically dropped unless contrastive, emphatic, or required for disambiguation:
  - Null subject: *Hablamos de lingüística.* (1st person plural implicit).
  - Overt contrastive: *Ella trabaja aquí, pero yo trabajo en Madrid.*
- **Flexible Constituent Ordering**:
  - **VSO / VOS** in interrogatives, unaccusative verbs (*Llegó el tren*), or presentational focus (*Compró el libro Juan*).
  - **Inverted Punctuation Invariant**: Questions and exclamations strictly require paired inverted opening punctuation: `¿...?` and `¡...!`.

## 2. Clitic Pronominal Syntax & Clitic Climbing
Spanish features an intricate clitic pronoun system with strict topological positioning:
- **Proclisis (Pre-verbal)**: Finite conjugated verbs demand proclitic position:
  - *Te lo doy.* (Indirect Object `te` precedes Direct Object `lo`).
- **Enclisis (Post-verbal Attachment)**: Non-finite forms (infinitives, gerunds) and affirmative imperatives demand enclisis:
  - Infinitivo: *dártelo* (accent added to preserve phonological stress).
  - Gerundio: *dándomelo*.
  - Imperativo afirmativo: *¡Dímelo!* (vs Imperativo negativo proclítico: *¡No me lo digas!*).
- **Spurious "Se" Rule**: When 3rd person indirect clitics (*le*, *les*) precede 3rd person direct clitics (*lo*, *la*, *los*, *las*), the indirect clitic converts morphophonologically to *se*:
  - *Le lo di* $\longrightarrow$ **Se lo di**.
- **Clitic Doubling**: Redundant dative clitics are standard and often mandatory when an indirect object is explicit (*Le entregué las llaves a María*).
- **Clitic Climbing**: In periphrastic verb constructions, clitics can climb to the auxiliary or remain enclitic to the non-finite verb:
  - *Lo quiero ver* $\iff$ *Quiero verlo*.

## 3. Mood Matrix: Indicative vs Subjunctive Selection
The Spanish mood system is ruled by syntactic-semantic selection across matrix and embedded subordinate clauses:
- **Indicative Mood**: Asserts factual reality, epistemic certainty, or high belief commitment (*Sé que viene hoy*).
- **Subjunctive Mood**: Mandated under semantic domains captured by the **WEIRDOS** rubric:
  - **W**ishes / Volition: *Quiero que estudies.*
  - **E**motion / Evaluation: *Me alegra que estés bien.*
  - **I**mpersonal expressions: *Es necesario que salgamos temprano.*
  - **R**ecommendations / Requests: *Te sugiero que leas este libro.*
  - **D**oubt / Denial / Negation: *Dudo que sea verdad.* / *No creo que venga.* (vs Affirmative *Creo que viene*).
  - **O**jalá / Hope: *Ojalá llueva pronto.*
  - **S**ubordinate Adverbial & Relative clauses of non-existent/unidentified referents: *Busco un libro que tenga soluciones.* (vs *Busco el libro que tiene soluciones*).

---

## 4. Personal "A" (Differential Object Marking)
- **Rule**: Direct objects that denote specific, identified human beings or anthropomorphized entities require the preposition **a**:
  - Direct object human: *Veo a Carlos.*
  - Direct object inanimate: *Veo el coche.*
  - Indefinite non-specific: *Necesito un médico.* (any doctor) vs *Busco al médico de guardia.* (specific doctor).
