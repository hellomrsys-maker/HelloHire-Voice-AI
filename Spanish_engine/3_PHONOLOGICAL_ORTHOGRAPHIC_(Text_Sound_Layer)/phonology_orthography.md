# Layer 3: Phonological & Orthographic Layer — Spanish Engine

## 1. Vowel System & Syllable Architecture
- **5 Pure Vowels**: `/a/`, `/e/`, `/i/`, `/o/`, `/u/`.
  - No vowel reduction to schwa `[ə]`: unstressed vowels preserve crisp acoustic form.
- **Diphthongs and Hiatus**:
  - Diphthong: Strong vowel (`a, e, o`) + weak vowel (`i, u`) or two weak vowels (*tierra*, *fuego*, *ciudad*).
  - Hiatus: Two strong vowels separated into distinct syllables (*te-a-tro*, *po-e-ma*) or a stressed weak vowel (*pa-ís*, *dí-a*, *ba-úl*).

## 2. Consonantal Phonology & Dialect Variations
- **Rhotics (Contrastive Vibrants)**:
  - Alveolar tap `/ɾ/`: Single intervocalic *r* (*caro* - expensive).
  - Alveolar trill `/r/`: Double *rr* (*carro* - car) and word-initial *r* (*rosa*).
- **Seseo vs Distinción**:
  - *Distinción* (Peninsular standard): Letters *z*, *ce*, *ci* pronounced as voiceless dental fricative `/θ/`, while *s* is `/s/` (*caza* `/ˈkaθa/` vs *casa* `/ˈkasa/`).
  - *Seseo* (Latin America, Canaries, southern Spain): Both *z/c* and *s* realized as `/s/`.
- **Yeísmo**: Neutralization of `/ʎ/` (*ll*) and `/ʝ/` (*y*) into `/ʝ/` or affricate `/dʒ/` (and Rioplatense `/ʃ/` or `/ʒ/`).

## 3. Lexical Stress & Accentuation Rules (Acentuación RAE)
Spanish follows strict deterministic accentuation rules governed by the Royal Spanish Academy (RAE):

| Stress Pattern | Stressed Syllable | Tilde (Written Accent) Rule | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Aguda** | Last syllable | Carries tilde if ending in **vocal**, **-n**, or **-s** | *can-ción*, *ca-fé*, *a-mor*, *re-loj* |
| **Llana (Grave)** | Penultimate syllable | Carries tilde if ending in **consonant other than -n, -s** | *ár-bol*, *fá-cil*, *me-sa*, *jo-ven* |
| **Esdrújula** | Antepenultimate syllable | **Always** carries tilde | *mú-si-ca*, *rá-pi-do*, *gra-má-ti-ca* |
| **Sobreesdrújula** | Pre-antepenultimate | **Always** carries tilde (enclitic clitic chains) | *cóm-pra-me-lo*, *dán-do-se-lo* |

### 3.1 Tilde Diacrítica (Disambiguating Accent)
Distinguishes homophonic monosyllables and function words:
- *él* (personal pronoun) vs *el* (definite article).
- *tú* (subject pronoun) vs *tu* (possessive determiner).
- *mí* (prepositional pronoun) vs *mi* (possessive determiner).
- *sí* (affirmative adverb / reflexive) vs *si* (conditional conjunction).
- *sé* (verb *saber* / *ser*) vs *se* (reflexive/passive clitic).
- *dé* (verb *dar*) vs *de* (preposition).
- *más* (comparative adverb) vs *mas* (archaic adversative conjunction).
- *té* (noun tea) vs *te* (clitic pronoun).
- Interrogatives/exclamatives with tilde: *qué*, *cuándo*, *dónde*, *cómo*, *quién*, *por qué*.

---

## 4. Orthographic Invariants
- **Inverted Punctuation**: Every interrogative clause opens with `¿` and closes with `?`; every exclamative opens with `¡` and closes with `!`.
- **Especial Character Set**: `ñ`, `á`, `é`, `í`, `ó`, `ú`, `ü` (dieresis in *güe*, *güi* e.g., *bilingüe*, *lingüística*).
