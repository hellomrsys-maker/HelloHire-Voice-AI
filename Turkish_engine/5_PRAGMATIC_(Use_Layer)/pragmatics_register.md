# Layer 5: Pragmatics, Register & Honorific Dynamics (Turkish Language Engine)

## 1. The T-V Distinction (*Sen* vs *Siz*) & Deictic Stratification

Turkish observes a strict second-person pronominal honorific binary reflecting social distance, hierarchy, and formality:

### 1.1 Second-Person Address Spectrum

| Pronoun | Grammatical Agreement | Pragmatic Sphere | Sociolinguistic Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sen** | 2nd Person Singular (*-sin*) | Intimate / Informal | Family members, close friends, peers, children, addressing pets. |
| **Siz** | 2nd Person Plural (*-siniz*) | Polite / Formal Singular | Unfamiliar adults, professional superiors, clients, teachers, doctors. |
| **Siz** (Plural) | 2nd Person Plural (*-siniz*) | Plural Address | Two or more individuals regardless of social hierarchy. |
| **Sizler** | 2nd Person Double Plural | Oratorical / Formal Plural | Public speeches, institutional addresses, ceremonial greetings. |

### 1.2 Agreement with Polite *Siz*
When addressing a single person respectfully as *Siz*:
1. **Verb Agreement**: Always inflected with the 2nd person plural ending:
   - *Geldiniz mi?* ("Did you arrive?")
   - *Nasıl yardımcı olabilirim?* ("How can I help you?")
2. **Predicate Adjective**: Remains in the singular or unmarked form:
   - *Çok naziksiniz.* ("You are very kind").

## 2. Honorific Titles & The Turkish Anthropomastic System

Unlike Western naming conventions which place honorifics before the surname (*Mr. Smith*), Turkish systematically places honorific titles **after the given name**:

```
[Given Name]  +  [Postpositive Honorific Title]  -->  Example
Ahmet             Bey                                  Ahmet Bey (Mr. Ahmet)
Ayşe              Hanım                                Ayşe Hanım (Ms. Ayşe)
Mehmet            Hocam                                Mehmet Hocam (My Professor/Teacher Mehmet)
```

### 2.1 Major Honorific Markers
- **Bey**: Respectful title for men (appended to first name: *Kemal Bey*).
- **Hanım**: Respectful title for women (appended to first name: *Fatma Hanım*).
- **Hocam** (*lit.* my teacher): Highly prevalent in academic, professional, and corporate peer settings to denote intellectual respect.
- **Efendim** (*lit.* my master): Polite vocative used to address customers, elders, or to say "Pardon me / Yes?".
- **Beyefendi / Hanımefendi**: Formal respectful terms when addressing a gentleman or lady whose name is unknown.

### 2.2 Socio-Cultural Well-Wishes (Kalıp İfadeler)
Turkish possesses formulaic courtesy expressions tailored to specific human situations:
- **Kolay gelsin** ("May it come easy"): Uttered to anyone currently working or undertaking labor.
- **Geçmiş olsun** ("May it be in the past"): Expressed to someone experiencing illness, hardship, or an accident.
- **Hayırlı olsun** ("May it be auspicious"): Congratulations upon acquiring property, a new job, or starting an endeavor.
- **Elinize sağlık** ("Health to your hands"): Thanking someone for cooking, crafting, or completing physical/intellectual work.

## 3. Epistolary Standards & Email Registers

### 3.1 Salutation Formulas
- **Formal Corporate / Institutional**:
  - *Sayın [Adı] Bey,* (Dear Mr. [Name],)
  - *Sayın [Adı] Hanım,* (Dear Ms. [Name],)
  - *Sayın Yetkili,* (To whom it may concern / Dear Authority,)
- **Academic / Intellectual**:
  - *Değerli Hocam,* (Dear Professor / Teacher,)
  - *Sayın Profesör Dr. [Soyadı],* (Dear Professor Dr. [Surname],)
- **Informal / Personal**:
  - *Sevgili [Adı],* (Dear [Name],)
  - *Merhaba [Adı],* (Hello [Name],)

### 3.2 Valediction Formulas
- **Formal Business**:
  - *Saygılarımla,* (With my respects,)
  - *İyi çalışmalar dilerim,* (I wish you productive work,)
  - *Bilgilerinize arz ederim.* (Submitted for your information - Official/bureaucratic).
- **Semi-Formal**:
  - *Görüşmek üzere,* (See you soon / Until we meet,)
  - *İyi günler dilerim,* (I wish you a good day,)
- **Informal**:
  - *Sevgilerle,* (With love,)
  - *Kendine iyi bak,* (Take good care of yourself,)
