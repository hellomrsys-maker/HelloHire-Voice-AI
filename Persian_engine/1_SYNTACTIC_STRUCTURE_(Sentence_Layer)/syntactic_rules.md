# Persian Syntactic Structure & Grammar Specifications (Sentence Layer)

## 1. Canonical Word Order: Subject-Object-Verb (SOV)
Persian (Farsi/Dari/Tajik) belongs to the Indo-Iranian branch of Indo-European. Unlike its Western Indo-European relatives (English, Spanish, French) which are strictly or predominantly SVO, Persian has a canonical **SOV (Subject - Object - Verb)** head-final clause topology:
$$\text{Sentence} = [\text{Subject}] + [\text{Prepositional Phrase / Indirect Object}] + [\text{Direct Object} + (rā)] + [\text{Verb}]$$

### Syntactic Examples:
- *کتاب را خواندم* (*ketāb rā khāndam*) = "I read the book." (O - V)
- *علی به دانشگاه رفت* (*'ali be dāneshgāh raft*) = "Ali went to university." (S - PP - V)
- *من دیروز یک سیب خوردم* (*man diruz yek sib khordam*) = "I ate an apple yesterday." (S - Adv - O - V)

## 2. Pro-Drop & Subject Ellipsis
Persian is an overt pro-drop language. Because finite verbs inflect unambiguously for person and number via six personal suffixes, overt subject pronouns (*man, to, u, mā, shomā, ānhā*) are omitted in unmarked utterances and only introduced for focus, topicalization, or emphatic contrast.

## 3. The Ezafe Construct (کسره اضافه - Eżāfe)
The Ezafe is a foundational enclitic morpheme (pronounced /-e/ after consonants, /-ye/ after vowels and silent *heh*) that links a nominal head to following attributive adjectives and possessive noun phrases:
1. **Attributive Modification**:
   - *کتابِ خوب* (*ketāb-e khub*) = "good book" (Noun-Head + *e* + Adj)
   - *دانشگاهِ بزرگ* (*dāneshgāh-e bozorg*) = "large university"
2. **Genitive / Possessive Modification**:
   - *خانهٔ علی* (*khāneh-ye 'ali*) = "Ali's house" (Noun-Head + *ye* + Possessor)
   - *کیفِ من* (*kif-e man*) = "my bag"
3. **Ezafe Chaining**:
   - *درِ خانهٔ بزرگِ ما* (*dar-e khāneh-ye bozorg-e mā*) = "the door of our big house"

## 4. Differential Object Marking (نشانه مفعول صریح - Rā / را)
Persian employs a postpositional particle **را** (*rā*) for Differential Object Marking (DOM):
- **Definite Direct Objects**: Obligatorily take *rā*:
  - *کتاب را خریدم* (*ketāb rā kharidam*) = "I bought **the** book."
- **Indefinite Specific Direct Objects**: Take indefinite *-i* followed by *rā*:
  - *کتابی را خریدم* (*ketābi rā kharidam*) = "I bought a [specific] book."
- **Non-Specific / Generic Direct Objects**: Must NOT take *rā*:
  - *کتاب خریدم* (*ketāb kharidam*) = "I bought books / I did book-buying."

## 5. Light Verb Constructions (افعال مرکب - Complex Predicates)
Over 90% of active verbal expressions in contemporary Persian are formed by combining a non-verbal element (noun, adjective, or prepositional phrase) with a light auxiliary verb:
- *کار کردن* (*kār kardan*) = "to work" (literally "work do")
- *صحبت کردن* (*sohbat kardan*) = "to speak"
- *پیدا کردن* (*peydā kardan*) = "to find"
- *تسلیم شدن* (*taslim shodan*) = "to surrender" (literally "surrender become")
- *حرکت کردن* (*harekat kardan*) = "to move"

## 6. Prepositions & Postpositions
- Persian uses prepositions for most spatial/temporal cases: *be* (to), *az* (from), *dar* (in), *bā* (with), *barāye* (for).
- *rā* is the unique postposition in modern standard Persian.
