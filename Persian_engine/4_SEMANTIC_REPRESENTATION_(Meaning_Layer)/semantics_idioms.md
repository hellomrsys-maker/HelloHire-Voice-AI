# Persian Semantic Representation & Idiomatic Space (Meaning Layer)

## 1. Light Verb Semantics (Complex Predicate Deconstruction)
In Persian, complex predicates distribute semantic load between the non-verbal nominal/adjectival host and the light verb vector:

| Light Verb | Primary Semantic Feature | Productive Examples |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **کردن** (*kardan*) | Causative / Agentive dynamic action | *kār kardan* (to work), *bāz kardan* (to open) |
| **شدن** (*shodan*) | Inchoative / Passive transition | *bāz shodan* (to get opened), *gom shodan* (to get lost) |
| **زدن** (*zadan*) | Percussive / Repetitive contact | *harf zadan* (to talk), *labkhand zadan* (to smile) |
| **دادن** (*dādan*) | Transfer / Emission | *feshār dādan* (to press), *anjām dādan* (to perform) |
| **گرفتن** (*gereftan*) | Acquisition / Inception | *yād gereftan* (to learn), *ārām gereftan* (to calm down) |
| **کشیدن** (*keshidan*) | Protracted endurance / Drawing | *dard keshidan* (to suffer pain), *entezār keshidan* (to wait) |
| **خوردن** (*khordan*) | Patientive absorption / Ingestion | *zamin khordan* (to fall down), *ghosse khordan* (to grieve) |

## 2. Metaphorical Body Idioms (اندام‌واژه‌ها)
Persian classical and modern discourse makes prolific use of somatotopic metaphors:
- **چشم** (*cheshm* - eye):
  - *cheshm-e man* (on my eyes / gladly)
  - *cheshm-pūshi kardan* (to overlook / turn a blind eye)
  - *cheshm-rūshani* (housewarming gift)
- **دل** (*del* - heart/stomach):
  - *del-tang shodan* (to miss someone / feel homesick)
  - *del-sūz* (compassionate / caring)
  - *del be del rāh dārad* (hearts find a way to each other)
- **دست** (*dast* - hand):
  - *dast-o-del-bāz* (generous / open-handed)
  - *az dast dādan* (to lose)
  - *dast-res* (access / available)

## 3. Classical Literature & Proverbial Wisdom (ضرب‌المثل‌ها)
Persian prose and formal discourse embeds proverbs codified by Saadi, Hafez, Rumi, and Ferdowsi:
- *هر چه کنی به خود کنی گر همه نیک و بد کنی*
  (*har che koni be khod koni gar hame nik o bad koni*)
  "Whatever you do, good or bad, you do unto yourself."
- *جوجه را آخر پاییز می‌شمارند*
  (*juje rā ākhar-e pāyīz mi-shomārand*)
  "Don't count your chickens before they hatch." (Literally: "They count chicks at the end of autumn.")
- *با یک گل بهار نمی‌شود*
  (*bā yek gol bahār nemi-shavad*)
  "One swallow does not make a summer." (Literally: "One flower does not make spring.")
