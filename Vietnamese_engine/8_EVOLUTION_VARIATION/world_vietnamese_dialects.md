# World Vietnamese Dialects & Diachronic Evolution (Variation Layer)

## 1. Dialectal Trichotomy of Vietnam
Vietnamese exhibits sharp dialectal variation across three major geographic regions, primarily distinguished by phonological mergers and vocabulary:

1. **Northern Dialect (Tiếng Bắc - Hanoi Standard)**:
   - Official national radio/television and educational prestige standard.
   - Retains all **6 phonemic tones** distinctly (Ngang, Huyền, Sắc, Hỏi, Ngã, Nặng).
   - Onset mergers: /d/, /gi/, /r/ merge into [z]; /ch/ and /tr/ merge into [tɕ]; /x/ and /s/ merge into [s].
2. **Central Dialect (Tiếng Trung - Hue / Nghệ An / Quảng Bình)**:
   - Phonologically archaic; preserves distinctive retroflex onsets (/tr/, /r/).
   - Merges tone *ngã* into *hỏi*, producing a 5-tone or 4-tone system.
   - Highly distinctive regional vocabulary: *mô, tê, răng, rứa* instead of *đâu, kia, sao, thế*.
3. **Southern Dialect (Tiếng Nam - Saigon / Mekong Delta)**:
   - Dynamic commercial and diaspora standard (~40M speakers).
   - Merges tone *ngã* and *hỏi* into a single dipping-rising tone (5 tones total).
   - Initial consonants: /v/ is pronounced as glide [j] (*về* $\to$ *dề*); retroflexes /tr, ch/ distinguished.
   - Coda mergers: Final *-t* and *-c* merge after front vowels (*ít* and *ích* sound identical).
   - Vocabulary differences: *ly* (glass) vs North *cốc*; *thìa* (spoon) vs South *muỗng*; *xe gắn máy* (motorbike) vs North *xe máy*.

## 2. Diachronic Evolution & Script Shift
1. **Chữ Nôm (𡨸喃)**:
   - Historical logographic script adapting Chinese characters to write native Vietnamese morphemes from the 10th to early 20th century.
2. **Chữ Quốc Ngữ (Latinization)**:
   - Created in the 17th century by Jesuit missionaries (notably Alexandre de Rhodes, *Dictionarium Annamiticum Lusitanum et Latinum* 1651).
   - Officially codified as the sole national script by decree in the early 20th century.
