# Layer 1: Syntactic Structure (Sentence Layer) - Bengali Engine (বাংলা)

## 1. Primary Word Order Typology
Bengali exhibits strict **SOV (Subject - Object - Verb)** canonical word order. It is a strictly head-final, left-branching language within the Eastern Indo-Aryan (Magadhan) subfamily.

```
Canonical Clause Architecture:
[Subject / Topic] + [Indirect Object] + [Direct Object] + [Adverbial / Postpositional Phrase] + [Finite Verb Complex] + [Negative Particle]
```

### Core Syntactic Exemplars:
- *আমি বই পড়ছি।* (`Ami boi porchi`) - "I am reading a book" (Subject - Direct Object - Verb)
- *তিনি আমাকে একটি চিঠি দিলেন।* (`Tini amake ekti chithi dilen`) - "He gave me a letter" (Subject - Indirect Object - Direct Object - Verb)
- *সে কাল কলকাতায় যাবে।* (`Se kal kolkatay jabe`) - "He will go to Kolkata tomorrow" (Subject - Temporal Adv - Locative PP - Verb)

---

## 2. Non-Ergative Alignment (Consistent Nominative-Accusative)
Unlike Western Indo-Aryan languages (such as Hindustani/Hindi-Urdu, Marathi, Gujarati, Punjabi), **Bengali possesses NO split-ergative alignment**.
- The subject remains in the **nominative case** in ALL tenses and aspects (past, present, future, perfective, imperfective).
- The finite verb ALWAYS agrees with the subject in person (1st, 2nd, 3rd) and honorific tier.
- No ergative postposition (such as Hindi *ne*) exists in standard Bengali.
  - Correct: *রাম ভাত খেল।* (`Ram bhat khelo`) - "Ram ate rice" (*Ram* is nominative, *khelo* agrees with 3rd-person *Ram*).
  - Contrast with Hindi: *Ram ne bhat khaya* (ergative *ne*).

---

## 3. Four-Case System & Nominal Declension
Bengali nouns and pronouns inflect for four distinct grammatical cases:

| Case | Marker (Consonant Stems) | Marker (Vowel Stems) | Functional Role | Exemplar |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nominative** | -Ø (unmarked) | -Ø / -e (collective/agent) | Subject of clause | *ছাত্র পড়ে* (`chatro pore`), *লোকে বলে* (`loke bole`) |
| **Objective (Acc/Dat)** | -ke | -ke | Specific/animate direct & indirect objects | *ছেলেটিকে ডাকো* (`cheletike dako`), *মাকে দাও* (`make dao`) |
| **Genitive** | -er | -r | Possessive, governor of postpositions | *বইয়ের পাতা* (`boier pata`), *ছেলের নাম* (`cheler naam`) |
| **Locative / Instrumental** | -e | -te / -y / -ye | Spatial/temporal location, instrument | *ঘরে* (`ghore`), *বাড়িতে* (`barite`), *ঢাকায়* (`dhakay`) |

### Differential Object Marking (DOM):
- **Animate / Definite Objects**: Marked with `-ke` (*আমি রাহুলকে দেখেছি* - `Ami Rahulke dekhechi`).
- **Inanimate / Indefinite Objects**: Unmarked (-Ø) (*আমি বই পড়ছি* - `Ami boi porchi`, NOT *\*Ami boike porchi*).

---

## 4. Postpositional Phrasal Structure
Bengali utilizes postpositions (*অনুসর্গ* - `Anusorgo`) that follow nominal arguments. Most postpositions obligatorily govern the **Genitive case** (-er / -r):

- *জন্য* (`jonno` - "for"): *আমার জন্য* (`amar jonno` - "for me")
- *সাথে / সঙ্গে* (`sathe / shonge` - "with"): *তার সাথে* (`tar sathe` - "with him")
- *কাছে* (`kache` - "near / to"): *বাবার কাছে* (`babar kache` - "near father")
- *সামনে* (`shamne` - "in front of"): *বাড়ির সামনে* (`barir shamne` - "in front of the house")
- *পরে* (`pore` - "after"): *কাজের পরে* (`kajer pore` - "after work")
- *দ্বারা / দিয়ে* (`dwara / diye` - "by / with"): Instrumental postposition.

---

## 5. Non-Finite Clause Chaining
Complex discourse relies on non-finite verbal participles chaining prior actions before the terminal matrix verb:
1. **Conjunctive Participle (`-e`)**: Expresses sequential action ("having done X, Y happened").
   - *সে ভাত খেয়ে স্কুলে গেল।* (`Se bhat kheye skule gelo`) - "Having eaten rice, he went to school."
2. **Purposive Infinitive (`-te`)**: Expresses purpose or complementation ("to do X").
   - *আমি গান গাইতে ভালোবাসি।* (`Ami gaan gaite bhalobashi`) - "I love to sing songs."
3. **Conditional Participle (`-le`)**: Expresses conditionality ("if X happens, Y").
   - *বৃষ্টি হলে আমি যাব না।* (`Brishti hole ami jabo na`) - "If it rains, I will not go."

---

## 6. Sentence-Final Negation Syntax
Unlike English (pre-verbal *do not*) and Hindi (pre-verbal *nahiñ*), Bengali standard negation places the negative marker **after the finite verb**:
- General Negation: *আমি জানি না।* (`Ami jani na`) - "I do not know."
- Past Completion Negation: *সে যায় নি।* (`Se jae ni`) - "He did not go" (contracted past negation replacing perfective + *na*).
- Equational Negative Copula: *তিনি শিক্ষক নন।* (`Tini shikkhok non`) - "He is not a teacher."
- Existential Negative Copula: *ঘরে কেউ নেই।* (`Ghore keu nei`) - "There is no one in the room."
