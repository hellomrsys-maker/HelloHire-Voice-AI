# World French Dialects & Diachronic Evolution (Variation Layer)

## 1. Major World French Varieties
1. **Metropolitan French (France / Paris standard)**:
   - Reference standard for European administrative, media, and academic discourse.
   - Distinctive merger of /œ̃/ (*un*) into /ɛ̃/ (*brin*).
   - Dropping of *ne* in casual colloquial registers (*j'sais pas*).
2. **Québécois / Laurentian French (Canada)**:
   - Conservation of /œ̃/ vs /ɛ̃/ distinction (*un brin*).
   - Affrication of dental stops /t/ and /d/ before high front vowels (/ts/ and /dz/: *tu* -> [tsy], *dire* -> [dziʁ]).
   - Diphthongization of long vowels in closed syllables (*fête* -> [faɛ̯t]).
   - Distinctive interrogative marker *-tu* (*tu viens-tu ?*).
   - Strict preservation of traditional French terminology against anglicisms (*courriel*, *divulgâcher*, *fin de semaine*).
3. **African French (Francophone West & Central Africa)**:
   - Spoken by over 160 million speakers across Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire, DRC, Cameroon.
   - Ivorian French (*Nouchi* slang): rich hybridization with Akan and Mande languages.
   - Tendency toward fully articulated final consonants and distinct rhythmic cadences.
4. **Swiss & Belgian French**:
   - Distinctive vigesimal numeral replacements: *septante* (70) and *nonante* (90); in Swiss French also *huitante* or *octante* (80).
   - Belgian lexical items (*déjeuner* = breakfast, *dîner* = lunch, *souper* = dinner).

## 2. Diachronic Evolution
- **Old French (9th - 14th c.)**: Two-case system (cas sujet vs cas régime), V2 word order, free pro-drop, diphthong-rich inventory.
- **Middle French (14th - 17th c.)**: Loss of case inflections, fixation of SVO order, loss of pro-drop.
- **Classical French (17th - 18th c.)**: Académie Française standardization, codification of liaison and past participle agreement.
- **Contemporary French (21st c.)**: Rapid verlan lexical innovations (*cimer, meuf, chelou*), expansion of inclusive writing (*point médian*), digital text contraction.
