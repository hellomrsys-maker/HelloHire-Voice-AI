# French Semantic Representation & Idioms (Meaning Layer)

## 1. Aspectual Contrasts: *Passé Composé* vs. *Imparfait*
Unlike English simple past, French maintains a strict aspectual bifurcation in narrative discourse:
- **Passé Composé (Aspect perfectif / ponctuel)**:
  - Completed actions, sequential events in a plot, bounded duration, sudden interruptions.
  - *Soudain, il est tombé.* / *J'ai vécu trois ans à Paris.*
- **Imparfait (Aspect imperfectif / duratif / itératif)**:
  - Ongoing states, background description, habitual actions, unbounded psychological processes.
  - *Il faisait beau et les oiseaux chantaient.* / *Quand j'étais petit, je lisais tous les soirs.*

## 2. Pre-nominal vs. Post-nominal Adjectival Semantics
Most French adjectives follow the noun (distinguishing/objective attributes). A small subset (BAGS: Beauty, Age, Goodness, Size) precede:
- *Un beau tableau, une vieille maison, un bon livre, un grand garçon.*

### Semantic Shift Adjectives based on Position:
| Adjective | Pre-nominal (Figurative / Subjective) | Post-nominal (Literal / Objective) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *grand* | *un grand homme* (a great man) | *un homme grand* (a tall man) |
| *pauvre* | *un pauvre homme* (a pitiful/unfortunate man) | *un homme pauvre* (a penniless man) |
| *ancien* | *mon ancien professeur* (my former teacher) | *un meuble ancien* (an antique piece of furniture) |
| *propre* | *ma propre chambre* (my own room) | *une chambre propre* (a clean room) |
| *cher* | *mon cher ami* (my dear friend) | *un livre cher* (an expensive book) |
| *curieux* | *une curieuse histoire* (a strange story) | *un enfant curieux* (an inquisitive child) |

## 3. High-Frequency Idiomatic Expressions
- *Il y a*: "There is / there are" (locative/existential) or "ago" (temporal: *il y a trois jours*).
- *Avoir besoin de*: "To need".
- *Avoir envie de*: "To feel like / to want".
- *Avoir peur de*: "To be afraid of".
- *Faire semblant de*: "To pretend".
- *Se rendre compte de*: "To realize".
- *Être en train de + inf*: Progressive aspect equivalent ("to be in the middle of doing").
- *Venir de + inf*: Recent past ("to have just done").
